隷
Uncategorized
Kun: tsuku, shimobe, shitagau  |  On: rei
to attach ・ subordinate servant ・ bondservant ・ to follow

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 隷 through a dangerous spirit-bearing animal, a cloth, and the hand holding that cloth. The related simpler graph 隶 originally represents the cloth in the hand. In the fuller composition the cloth touches the charged animal and takes up its harmful power.

This is a rite of transferring affliction: the curse or misfortune is made to adhere to another bearer. A person bearing that transferred condition can then be dedicated as a servant of the deity. Shirakawa follows this into classical references to the permanent servants of a shrine or sacred establishment.

The sequence therefore joins attachment, transferred ritual burden, and service. From the person assigned to such service come the general meanings servant, subordinate, and following another. He rejects a phonetic explanation through 柰 because the historical sounds do not agree, and instead organizes the graph through the transfer action represented by its components.
