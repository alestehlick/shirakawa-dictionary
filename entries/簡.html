簡
Kun: erabu, fuda  |  On: kan
simplicity ・ brevity ・ letter ・ bamboo slip ・ to abridge

Explanation

簡 combines 竹, bamboo, with 間, also written 閒, as its sound element. A bamboo piece was heated over a flame, flattened, and prepared as a thin strip on which characters could be written. Such strips were 簡; bound together, they made a written text. Wooden strips could be named in the same way.

The character therefore means a writing strip, document, or letter. Compared with writing on jade or silk, this was a plain and relatively simple medium. 簡 developed the senses ‘simple,’ ‘brief,’ and ‘abridged,’ then omitting elaboration or treating something with little ceremony.

Its uses for selecting, checking, or comparing come through the related 柬, a graph concerned with choosing what is inside a sack.
