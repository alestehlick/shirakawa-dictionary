反
Kun: soru, sorasu, somuku, kaesu, kaeru  |  On: han, hon, tan
oppose ・ to rebel ・ to turn back ・ to bend back

Explanation

反 combines 厂, a cliff, with 又, a hand. It shows a hand taking hold of the steep face to climb upward. A slope of this kind is 坂; when it belongs to a sacred place, it can be written 阪 with the divine-ladder element 阜.

Attempting to climb into such a protected sacred place was an act of defiance. 反 therefore came to express rebellion, opposing authority, and breaking faith. Some bronze forms add the earth-deity sign beneath the cliff, making the sacred setting clearer.

The graph also has early uses for returning or giving something back. Its later meanings include reversing, overturning, turning back, and repeating an action. The climbing hand supplies the original form, while these uses gather around opposition and return.
