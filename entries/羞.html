羞
Uncategorized
Kun: susumeru, tabemono, haji, hajiru  |  On: shuu
to present an offering ・ sacrificial food ・ gift ・ shame or embarrassment

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 羞 as a sacrificial sheep, 羊, held out by a hand. Although the received lower element resembles 丑, the oracle-bone and bronze forms show the ordinary hand 又, which he takes as the starting point.

The action is presenting sheep meat to a deity. From this come an offering, prepared food, and a gift made in formal presentation. The older definition of advancing or offering something preserves that action.

For shame and embarrassment, Shirakawa does not simply accept the proposal of a borrowing from 醜. He suggests that a person's shame could itself be presented before the deity, comparing language about becoming an ancestor's disgrace. This is his bridge between ritual presentation and the moral or emotional sense of 羞.
