串
Kun: kushi, tsuranuku  |  On: kan
to thread through ・ skewer in Japanese ・ string of objects ・ to become familiar by related usage

Explanation

串 pictures objects pierced or threaded on a single line. Shirakawa compares an early form related to 貫, showing cowries pierced and strung on a cord, and suggests that 串 represents one strand rather than the paired arrangement called 朋.

This identification is proposed through their shared sound and meaning. An early Book of Odes occurrence appears in a people's name, so that passage is not by itself a literal description of skewering food.

The character overlaps with 貫 for passing through and with 慣 and related writings for familiarity or habituation. Japanese uses it concretely for a skewer and skewered things. The material threading scene and the later sound-related uses should therefore be kept distinct.
