稽
Uncategorized
Kun: itaru, todomaru, kangaeru  |  On: kei
to examine ・ to consider ・ to detain ・ to bow deeply ・ practice in 稽古

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 稽 through a sacred military entrance. The component resembling 禾 belongs, in his analysis, to the paired wooden markers set before a camp, each carrying a crosspiece. As boundary markers protecting a sacred place, these could receive a dog sacrifice; 尤 represents the dead dog in this composition.

He connects the lower element, now resembling 旨, with early graphs for a deity's arrival and the worshipper's reception of that arrival. Bronze inscriptions use the related form in 稽首, the profound bow. This gives the whole graph a setting of welcoming, bowing before, and retaining a divine presence.

The next step is consulting the divine intention. From considering or testing something against that intention comes the ordinary sense of examining and pondering. 稽古, examining or studying what is ancient, belongs to this later extension. The argument follows the sanctuary, its reception rite, and the development into reflective inquiry.
