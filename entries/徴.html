徴
Kun: mesu, shirushi, arawareru  |  On: chou, chi
to demand or summon ・ to punish ・ sign or evidence ・ to manifest

Explanation

徴 is the modern form of 徵. Shirakawa reads the older graph as a long-haired ritual specialist struck on a road, represented by 彳 and 攴. The person might be an elder or a female medium; exceptional ritual power is the important feature of his interpretation.

The beating is intended to exert punitive force on an enemy and compel the desired result. A manifested result then becomes the 徴験, the sign that the act has worked. Shirakawa compares 微 and 徼 as related coercive ritual formations.

He treats 徵 as an initial writing of 懲. From compelling and punishing come demanding, summoning, and exacting something; from the visible effect come a sign, proof, and manifestation. The older dictionary's simple summon gloss therefore records only one branch of his proposed ritual history.
