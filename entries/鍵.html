鍵
Kun: kagi, kusabi  |  On: ken
key ・ lock or fastening ・ lifting bar ・ axle wedge

Explanation

鍵 combines 金 with 建 as the phonetic ken. Shirakawa begins with older implement meanings: a bar passed through the two ears of a tripod vessel to lift it, and a wedge securing the end of a wheel axle.

The lock or key meaning is explained through borrowing for a sound-related word, not as a literal picture of a modern key. Shirakawa compares related vocabulary for barriers, tubes, and the complementary parts of a fastening.

Over time the lock-related sense becomes central. Japanese also applies 鍵 to the keys of a piano. The history therefore includes several kinds of inserted or securing implement, but Shirakawa explicitly retains the role of sound-based interchange when accounting for the transition to the familiar key and lock.
