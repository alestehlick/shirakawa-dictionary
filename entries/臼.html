臼
Kun: usu  |  On: kyuu
mortar for pounding ・ to pound in a mortar

Explanation

臼 is a pictograph of a pounding mortar. The older explanation describes a progression from a hollow in the ground to mortars hollowed from wood or stone, and interprets the internal marks as grain.

Shirakawa cautions that a mortar must be distinguished from a pitfall, even where dictionary classification groups similar shapes together. A trap into which a person falls and a vessel for pounding grain are different objects.

He makes a similar distinction for the lower part of the older form of 旧, which he interprets as a device holding a decoy bird rather than as 臼. His account therefore begins with the recognizable utensil but resists reading every superficially similar hollow shape as the same component.
