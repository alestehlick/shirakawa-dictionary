蜜
Uncategorized
Kun: hachimitsu  |  On: mitsu
honey ・ sweet nectar ・ honey-like sweetness

Explanation

Shirakawa begins with the fuller old writing recorded in the Shuowen, alongside 蜜 as a variant. He interprets that fuller composition as bees gathered densely inside a hive. The scene joins the insects, their enclosure, and their close concentration, giving a concrete setting to the sweet substance they produce.

He regards 蜜 itself as a semantic–phonetic formation continuing the sound and the association with dense gathering. This helps explain its closeness to 密, and the occasional interchange of the two writings. The primary ordinary meaning is the bees' honey, not sweetness in the abstract.

Shirakawa also records honey's use in preserving the dead and the making of wax seals or seal objects for funerary gifts. Those are historical applications of bee products, rather than separate origins of the character. His etymological sequence remains hive and clustered bees, the honey they yield, and the material's later practical and ritual uses.
