頓
Kun: tsumazuku, taoreru, kurushimu  |  On: ton
to bow to the ground ・ to stumble ・ to halt ・ suddenly

Explanation

Shirakawa analyzes 頓 as semantic–phonetic, with 頁 supplying the head and 屯 the sound ton. He reads 屯 through tied or bundled thread ends, carrying an association with reaching an end or stop.

The old definition concerns lowering the head. In 頓首 the seated worshipper touches the ground with the head, a bow listed among the formal kinds of obeisance. Shirakawa associates its original setting particularly with mourning and surrender, distinguishing it from other bowing formulas used in auspicious bronze inscriptions.

From the head's downward movement and the idea of coming to a stop develop falling, stumbling, checking, and halting. Abruptness or suddenness belongs to the later range, while dullness involves interchange with 鈍. His account keeps the concrete bow, the stopping association of the phonetic, and those subsequent verbal and adverbial uses connected.
