怨
Kun: uramu  |  On: en, on
resentment ・ to resent ・ grievance ・ sorrow

Explanation

Shirakawa identifies the phonetic in 怨 as 夗, a seated or crouching human figure, combined with 心 to locate the condition in the heart. He relates that posture to 宛, which he interprets as a person seated within a building in prayer. The shared posture and sound place 怨 within a group involving supplication.

The particular emotion is the troubled state of someone with an unresolved sorrow or grievance, turning toward prayer or appeal. It is from this afflicted, petitioning heart that Shirakawa develops the senses of resentment, sorrow, and angry hatred.

He also notes a seal-script variant containing 令. In his account 令 concerns receiving a divine command, so that variant reinforces the religious setting of appeal and response. The entry is consequently more specific than a simple equation of 心 with anger: it follows a posture of prayer into the feeling that prompts an aggrieved appeal.
