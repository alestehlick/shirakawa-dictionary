妖
Kun: ayashii, adeyaka  |  On: you
uncanny ・ bewitching ・ alluring ・ supernatural apparition

Explanation

The older writing combines 女 with 芺, an early form of 笑 that supplies the sound. This phonetic depicts a female medium dancing with raised hands. 夭, visible in the shorter 妖, expresses the bending, supple posture of that dance. The graceful figure connects the character with an alluring or captivating appearance.

The same medium could become possessed and utter an oracle. Such speech could be received as a harmful portent, giving 妖 its associations with uncanny events, malign wonders, and spirits. The related 若 expresses following the divine will; 妖 carries the more troubling implications of seductive display and potentially harmful ritual power.

The senses ‘bewitching’ and ‘supernatural’ thus belong to the same field of bodily grace, attraction, and spirit possession. The word can describe a person's beauty as well as an uncanny manifestation or being.
