妖
Uncategorized
Kun: ayashii, adeyaka  |  On: you
uncanny ・ bewitching ・ alluring ・ supernatural apparition

Explanation

Shirakawa begins with the fuller ancient writing, which he classifies as a semantic–phonetic formation. Its phonetic is an early form of 笑, interpreted as a female ritual medium dancing with raised hands. The related 夭 expresses the supple, bending posture of that dance. This connects the character's sound with an appearance at once graceful and arresting.

He then places the dancer in the setting of spirit possession and oracular speech. A medium's utterances could be regarded as ominous or harmful, and the character acquired associations with uncanny manifestations, malign wonders, and spirits. He contrasts this development with 若, which in his account expresses conformity to a divine will.

The personal sense, a captivating or voluptuous appearance, belongs to the same semantic field: an attraction that can also suggest seductive display. Thus the modern senses of beauty and the supernatural preserve two developments of the character's ritual and bodily associations.
