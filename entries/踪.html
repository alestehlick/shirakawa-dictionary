踪
Uncategorized
Kun: ato  |  On: sou
footprint ・ track ・ trace ・ to follow a trail

Explanation

踪 combines 足, the foot classifier, with 宗 as the phonetic sō. Shirakawa treats it as a later writing, noting its absence from the earliest dictionaries discussed in the entry. The visible 宗 should consequently not be turned into a new ritual explanation for the whole character.

The established word concerns footprints and the trail left by movement, as in 踪迹. A track both records where someone has been and gives another person a route to follow. From that relation come the verbal senses of following and tracing.

踪 is associated with 蹤 as a conventional variant writing. Shirakawa's account here is principally lexical and phonetic: the foot component identifies tracks, while the sound element connects the graph with its word. He does not offer an independent ancient pictorial origin for this later form.
