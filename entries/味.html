味
Kun: aji, ajiwai, ajiwau  |  On: mi, bi
taste ・ flavor ・ savor

Explanation

味 combines 口, the mouth, with 未 as its phonetic. 未 pictures a tree with luxuriant branches. The tender new growth at their tips has a particularly fine flavour, connecting the sound element with nourishment and savour.

The mouth receives that flavour. 味 therefore names taste, including the traditional five flavours, and the act of tasting or appreciating something. Savour belongs to the natural thing and is discovered through attending to it.

Tasting becomes a way of understanding more closely. A work, expression, or experience can have a distinctive 味: the quality or interest that emerges as one dwells on it. Physical taste and considered appreciation share this receptive act of finding what something contains.
