戚
Kun: ono, masakari, ureeru, shitashimu  |  On: seki
ceremonial axe ・ sorrow ・ anxiety ・ relatives ・ intimate connection

Explanation

Shirakawa begins with an axe. He identifies the phonetic element through the head of the implement and the radiating sheen of its blade, with 戈 making the weapon's character explicit. Early definitions and classical passages identify 戚 as an axe used together with a shield in martial dance.

Its ceremonial role is prominent: texts speak of jade axes and of decorated implements for the dance, and Shirakawa treats the object chiefly as ritual equipment in this setting. The axe meaning is therefore a concrete starting point supported by the named performance.

The senses sorrow, anxiety, or apprehension involve interchange with related words. He then suggests that intimate connection and relatives develop through sharing such sorrows. The familiar meaning in 親戚 belongs to that relational branch. His account distinguishes the axe's graphic origin from the sound-linked emotional vocabulary and its extension to one's own people.
