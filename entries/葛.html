葛
Kun: kuzu, kazura  |  On: katsu
kudzu ・ vine ・ kudzu-fibre cloth

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 葛 as semantic–phonetic: 艸 identifies a plant, and 曷 supplies the sound katsu. The concrete referent is the climbing kudzu vine and, by extension, climbing growth of that kind.

He follows the word into the use of its fibres. The old dictionary explanation names cloth made from the plant, and the Shijing's poem 葛覃 describes working the fibres into textiles and wearing the resulting garments. Shirakawa particularly associates the older material with ceremonial clothing.

The cloth could be coarse and was also suitable for summer wear. The character consequently names both the growing vine and the useful textile obtained from it. This is the main etymological progression he gives: a plant word formed with a phonetic, the plant's fibrous material, and garments manufactured from that material. The modern botanical and cloth senses continue those connected uses.
