呪
Uncategorized
Kun: norou, inoru  |  On: ju, shuu
to curse ・ to invoke ・ incantation ・ spell

Explanation

Shirakawa begins with 祝, the earlier writing that covered both benediction and imprecation. 呪 developed to mark the latter field more distinctly. He analyzes its received structure as 口 together with 兄. Here 兄 is the praying officiant presenting a sai, the ritual receptacle holding a written prayer, and the additional 口 belongs to the same world of spoken and deposited invocations.

Blessing and cursing are thus branches of a shared religious act: addressing powers through an efficacious formula. 呪 eventually specializes in curses, spells, and incantatory practice, while still retaining the broader sense of invoking or praying.

The graph is absent from the Shuowen; Shirakawa notes examples from the Later Han onward. He then follows the historical practice into offices concerned with incantations and into Japan, citing the Nihon shoki's account of a ritual specialist's arrival. This chronology distinguishes the later specialized graph from the older rite expressed by 祝.
