羨
Kun: urayamu, urayamashii, amari  |  On: sen, zen, en
to envy ・ to desire ・ surplus ・ passage into a tomb

Explanation

Shirakawa identifies the older saliva-related element as the phonetic in 羨. He finds the traditional explanation of its remaining components insufficient to account for the graph's formation and meaning.

He therefore proposes a connection among three attested uses: desire, surplus, and the approach passage into a tomb. Sacrificial meat could be offered in that passage and the remainder distributed to people. This proposed ritual setting gives the surplus meat and its desirability a common background.

His account also compares the phonetic's association with spitting and the related formation of 盗. The reconstruction is presented as a possibility, not a fully demonstrated scene. Envy and longing belong to one branch of the word, while the tomb-passage usage has its own reading and lexical connections.
