貌
Kun: katachi, kao, sugata, arawareru  |  On: bou
face ・ appearance ・ bearing ・ outward form

Explanation

Shirakawa begins with the simpler writing 皃. He interprets its upper 白 as the human head or face and the lower 儿 as the person presenting that appearance. The Shuowen records 貌 and another fuller form as alternatives.

The relevant appearance is formal bearing, particularly the countenance and posture displayed when appearing in a public or ceremonial setting. Shirakawa connects it with the vocabulary of ritual presentation associated with 頁 and 頌.

From that visible bearing come face, form, and appearance generally. A likeness can then be described through a shared outward form. The same external emphasis also permits a critical sense: a formally correct greeting may be only outward observance, without corresponding sincerity. The account follows the presented human face into both resemblance and the distinction between external manner and inner intention.
