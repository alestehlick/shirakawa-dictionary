艶
Uncategorized
Kun: tsuya, utsukushii, adeyaka  |  On: en
lustre ・ splendid beauty ・ alluring beauty ・ gloss

Explanation

Shirakawa explains the fuller formation through 豐 and 盍. 豐 represents grain piled in offering vessels, and 盍 a covered vessel. Together, in his interpretation, they express the beauty of a splendid presentation to the gods.

He questions the traditional treatment of 盍 as the phonetic because the sounds do not fit. His own account instead gives the carefully furnished offering a central role in the character's formation.

From the visual splendour of that sacred display, the word broadens to beauty, rich colour, and a lustrous appearance. It then becomes particularly associated with the attractive colour and appearance of a woman's face or figure. The later writings 艷 and 艶 carry this developed vocabulary of human beauty and allure. The progression therefore begins in a beautiful offering and moves into the sheen and captivating appearance of a person.
