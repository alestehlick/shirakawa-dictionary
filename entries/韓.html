韓
Kun: igeta  |  On: kan
Han, an ancient state ・ Korea ・ a framed well or its upright

Explanation

Shirakawa works back from 韓 to a bronze form depicting an upright pole. He identifies its principal element with a standard or flagstaff. The received 韋, associated with hide or leather, was not present in the bronze example he discusses; he suggests that leather could have been wrapped around such a pole when the fuller writing developed.

He also examines the Shuowen tradition, which explains the word through the framing of a well. An interpretation as a well mechanism would make the upright a post supporting a lifting device, and the leather element could then concern the leather vessel used to draw water. Shirakawa presents these possibilities rather than claiming a single fully secure original object.

The graph subsequently serves as the name of an ancient state. The country-name and modern Korea-related uses belong to that proper-name history. They should not be projected backward as a picture of the modern country in the character's components.
