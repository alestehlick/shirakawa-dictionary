彙
Kun: harinezumi, atsumaru  |  On: i
hedgehog ・ collection ・ group ・ category

Explanation

Shirakawa reads the earliest 彙 as a pictograph of a hedgehog curled into a rounded body. The Shuowen identifies the animal by comparison with a porcupine, but analyzes the graph through components and a shortened 胃 phonetic. Shirakawa instead takes the whole early shape as the animal itself.

The separate semantic–phonetic writing 蝟 also names the hedgehog. Through that associated graph and its sound-family, the word acquires the sense of things gathered densely or brought into a group. Shirakawa connects the family with 胃, 謂, and related words carrying a sense of close concentration.

From gathering together come a collection, a class, and a grouping of similar things. The use in 語彙, a body of vocabulary, follows this later sense. His explanation therefore moves from the depicted rounded animal to its related writings and the lexical development of collection and category.
