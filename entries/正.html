正
Kun: tadashii, tadasu, masa, masani  |  On: sei, shou
correct ・ right ・ to correct ・ proper

Explanation

正 combines a foot, 止, with the outline of a walled settlement. In the early forms the upper sign is an enclosure, later reduced to a single line. The foot moves toward the fortified place.

正 is the earlier writing for 征, campaigning and subduing. The conqueror's action was treated as justified, and the word extended to putting things right. Taking tribute from the conquered place belongs to 征; adding the striking implement 攴 produces 政, rule exercised through coercive action.

The person directing this order could also be called 正, an official or chief. From conquest and administration come proper order, correctness, justice, and purity. The modern top stroke preserves the abbreviated enclosure.
