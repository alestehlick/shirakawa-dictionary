塡
Uncategorized
Kun: fusagu, uzumeru, mitasu, hisashii  |  On: ten, chin
to bury ・ to fill ・ to plug or close ・ to make peaceful

Explanation

塡 combines 土 with 眞 as the phonetic ten, a reading also found in related graphs. Shirakawa interprets 眞 as the body of a person who died unnaturally and whose spirit was feared for its violent power.

In his account such a body was carefully buried and placed in a ritual structure to calm its anger. Filling and closing therefore belong initially to containing that dangerous presence. He compares 塞, whose packed ritual materials likewise shut harmful spirits in or out.

The ordinary senses of burying, plugging, adding material, and filling a space develop from this containment. Related 慎 and 鎮 concern respectful caution and pacification. Shirakawa's explanation makes the burial and calming rite central to a wider family of words, rather than beginning with neutral earth-moving alone.
