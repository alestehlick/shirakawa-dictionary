然
Kun: moeru, shikari, shikaredomo  |  On: zen, nen
so ・ thus ・ however ・ to burn ・ adverbial suffix

Explanation

然 joins 肰, dog meat, with 火, fire, now written as the four lower dots. As the meat is roasted, its fat catches fire. The character is the early writing of the burning word later distinguished as 燃.

Burning a dog was part of offerings to Heaven. The deity was believed to receive the sacrifice through its rising smell, giving the burning a particular ritual setting as well as a visible physical action.

The meanings ‘so,’ ‘thus,’ and ‘however,’ and the ending in words such as 突然, belong to sound-based interchange with grammatical words. They are a separate branch from roasting meat. Another 火 was added in 燃 to distinguish the burning sense while 然 continued in its extensive grammatical uses.
