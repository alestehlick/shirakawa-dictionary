萎
Kun: naeru, kareru, nayamu  |  On: i
to wither ・ to droop ・ to lose vitality ・ dried fodder

Explanation

萎 is semantic–phonetic, with 艸 identifying plants and 委 supplying the sound i. Shirakawa finds an association with pliancy or bending in 委 and its related words.

The older definition concerns fodder for cattle. Withered or dried vegetation can be gathered as animal feed, while the central descriptive action is the loss of freshness that makes a living plant droop and shrivel.

The same loss of vigour extends beyond plants. Through its relation with 痿, the word also concerns weakness, limpness, and a body or spirit deprived of vitality. Shirakawa places several related graphs in this sound-family of drooping and decline. The modern meaning of becoming dispirited therefore continues the image of life and colour fading from vegetation.
