蔑
Kun: sagesumu, arawasu, naigashironisuru, nai  |  On: betsu
to extinguish ・ to make nothing of ・ to despise ・ to display military merit

Explanation

Shirakawa interprets 蔑 through a ritual medium with ornamented eyes or brows and the killing action represented by 伐. In his account such women directed powerful invocations against an enemy during war. Captured enemy mediums could then be killed to extinguish their hostile spiritual force.

This gives the sense of making something cease to exist or reducing its power to nothing. He also connects the act with the display and recognition of military achievement, explaining an older honorific vocabulary involving 蔑暦. The related forms and their lower cutting element are important to his departure from the Shuowen's explanation through tired eyes.

From nullifying or reducing someone comes treating them as of little account, slighting, and despising. Shirakawa links this branch with related words for contempt and destruction. The apparently distant senses of military commendation and contempt are therefore traced through his reconstruction of wartime ritual power and its elimination.
