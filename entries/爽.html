爽
Kun: sawayaka, akiraka, tagau  |  On: sou
bright and beautiful ・ refreshing ・ clear ・ to differ or go astray

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 爽 as a frontal human figure, 大, with markings placed on both sides of the chest. He interprets these as red, breast-shaped designs applied to a woman's corpse to repel harmful spirits. Their protective beauty gives the senses bright and beautiful.

Oracle inscriptions applying the word to deceased royal women are important to his reconstruction. He rejects an explanation of the surrounding marks as a bright lattice window because it leaves the central person unexplained. Clarity and refreshing brightness develop from the beautiful ritual appearance.

The apparently opposite senses of error, difference, and loss require another route. Shirakawa notes sound-based interchange with 喪, lose or mourn, and considers discrepancy to have developed from that loss branch. They should not be derived from refreshing weather.
