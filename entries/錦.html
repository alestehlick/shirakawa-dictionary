錦
Kun: nishiki  |  On: kin
brocade ・ rich patterned textile ・ splendid ・ beautiful

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 錦 as semantic–phonetic. 帛 supplies the textile category and 金 the sound kin. The word names richly patterned woven fabric.

He follows that material through its regional production and tribute. The Shuowen associates it with patterned cloth from Xiangyi, where designs including tiger patterns were woven in the Han period. Other early references record woven tribute from the south, and a bronze inscription concerns the presentation of valuable fabric to the royal house.

These examples locate 錦 in the manufacture and exchange of prestigious textiles. The later adjective beautiful or splendid grows from the visible richness of the cloth. Shirakawa's explanation centres on a named kind of fabric and its historical use; the metal component's primary function in the graph is to carry the sound.
