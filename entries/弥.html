弥
Kun: ya, hisashii, iyoiyo  |  On: bi, mi
increasingly ・ ever more ・ long-lasting ・ extensive ・ to fill

Explanation

The modern 弥 abbreviates 彌. Shirakawa turns first to bronze inscriptions, where the graph combines 弓, a bow, a jewel-like element later resembling 日, and 爾. In his interpretation the bow is an implement for dispelling harmful powers, the jewel is auspicious, and 爾 represents decorative tattooing on a woman's upper body. Their combination probably expressed a rite seeking abundant blessings for her.

Bronze inscriptions use the character in wishes for prolonged life and a fortunate completion of one's years. This usage supplies the bridge from the ritual composition to duration, extension, fullness, and reaching an ultimate limit. From these senses come the adverbial meanings increasingly and ever more.

Shirakawa regards the later fuller form containing 長 as a graphic alteration. He therefore does not accept the Shuowen's analysis of that late form as sufficient evidence for the original construction. His interpretation of the bronze composition remains a proposed explanation, not a directly documented description of the ceremony.
