弥
Kun: ya, hisashii, iyoiyo  |  On: bi, mi
increasingly ・ ever more ・ long-lasting ・ extensive ・ to fill

Explanation

弥 abbreviates 彌. Bronze forms combine a bow, a jewel-shaped element, and 爾. The bow is a means of warding off harmful powers, the jewel is auspicious, and 爾 represents painted or tattooed decoration on a woman's upper body. Their combination probably concerns a rite seeking blessings and a fortunate life for her.

Bronze inscriptions use the word in wishes for long life and a favourable end to one's years. This provides the connection with duration: lasting a long time, extending through a whole span, and reaching its completion. The sense broadens to things extensive, far-reaching, full, or covering everything.

The adverbial uses ‘increasingly’ and ‘ever more’ belong to this extension. A later form adds 長, the long-haired human figure, extending the transmitted shape beyond the older bronze composition.
