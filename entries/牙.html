牙
Kun: kiba  |  On: ga, ge
fang ・ tusk ・ to bite ・ military headquarters

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 牙 as a pictograph of the upper and lower fangs meeting or interlocking. The old form represents the biting teeth themselves. He discusses the Shuowen's description of the powerful teeth and the later correction of its wording, keeping the origin in this concrete anatomical shape.

The first meanings are consequently fang, tusk, and biting with the fangs. Things resembling a projecting fang can also receive the name, extending the word from an animal's tooth to a similar shape.

Another development follows the fang-banner raised at the headquarters of a ruler or general. Through that emblem, 牙 comes to denote the headquarters itself and then an official establishment. Shirakawa compares the sound of 衙, associated with the military gate, in explaining this branch. The sequence joins the depicted tooth, a tooth-associated standard, and the place identified by that standard.
