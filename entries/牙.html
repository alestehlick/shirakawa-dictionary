牙
Kun: kiba  |  On: ga, ge
fang ・ tusk ・ to bite ・ military headquarters

Explanation

牙 pictures upper and lower fangs meeting or interlocking. The early form focuses on the opposing teeth and their bite. Its immediate meanings are a fang or tusk and the action of biting with such teeth.

The name extends to things shaped like a projecting fang. The character's sound family also includes notions of opposing or intersecting parts, while 芽 applies the related sound to an emerging shoot.

A fang-banner was raised at the headquarters of a ruler or general. Through this identifying emblem, 牙 could name the headquarters and then an official establishment. Its connection with 衙 belongs to this military-gate and administrative use.
