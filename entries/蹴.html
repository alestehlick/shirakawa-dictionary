蹴
Kun: keru, fumu, tsutsushimu  |  On: shuu, shuku
to kick ・ to tread ・ to rise suddenly ・ to straighten oneself respectfully

Explanation

蹴 combines 足 with 就 as the phonetic shū or shuku. The older definition concerns treading with the foot, while Shirakawa emphasizes a forceful movement like kicking as one suddenly rises.

Such an abrupt movement can also mark a change in demeanour. 蹴然 describes collecting oneself, straightening one's posture, or changing one's expression in respectful response. Physical movement and a sudden formal adjustment therefore meet in the same vocabulary.

Shirakawa compares related words for contraction, constraint, and solemn composure, as well as sudden action. The modern kicking sense is one part of that range. His account does not derive the whole character from the ordinary modern meaning accomplish of 就; 就 serves as the phonetic within this group of related action words.
