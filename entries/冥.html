冥
Uncategorized
Kun: kurai, fukai, haruka  |  On: mei, myou, men, ben
dark ・ unseen ・ deep ・ remote ・ the world of the dead

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 冥 as a picture of the cloth covering placed over a dead person's face. The graph represents the covering as a whole, with its lower strokes showing the hanging ties or cords. He connects this interpretation with the funeral face-cloth described in the Yili, including the cloth's contrasting surfaces and attached fastening cords.

The covering marks the deceased person's passage beyond ordinary sight. From this funerary setting he develops the meanings darkness and the unseen realm after death. Further extensions express something profound, hidden, distant, or beyond direct perception.

This differs from the Shuowen's explanation through 日, 六, and a covering, linked to the moon's waning after the sixteenth day. For Shirakawa that numerical decomposition misses the original object. Related forms such as 幎, the covering itself, and 瞑, closing the eyes, preserve the connection between a covered face, the cessation of sight, and darkness.
