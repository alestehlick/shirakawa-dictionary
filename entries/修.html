修
Kun: osameru, osamaru  |  On: shuu, shu
to repair ・ to cultivate ・ to master ・ to discipline ・ to purify

Explanation

修 combines 攸 with 彡. In the old form of 攸, water is poured down a person’s back. This is ritual washing, an ablution that removes impurity from the body. The added 彡 marks the cleansed and ordered condition produced by the washing.

Purifying the body also prepares the mind. The related 悠 adds 心, the heart, for the calm that follows purification. 修 develops the active side of this process: putting oneself in order, refining one’s conduct, and cultivating the abilities or learning needed for a task.

The same work of bringing something into a proper condition can be applied to objects and undertakings. 修 consequently means to repair, improve, complete, or carry out something with care. Study and self-cultivation retain the original emphasis on preparing and refining the person.
