丼
Kun: donburi, don  |  On:
older form of 井 ・ donburi bowl in Japanese

Explanation

Shirakawa discusses 丼 under 井, as an older well-frame form, rather than in a separate headword article. He reads 井 as a picture of the surrounding frame. The seal form 丼 includes a central dot, which the traditional explanation identifies as a vessel.

He cautions that similar early frame shapes also represented shackles, casting moulds, and pitfalls, later distinguished in other characters. The later account of well-field land division cannot simply be projected back into the period when the graph arose.

His entry separately records Japanese donburi, a bowl. That lexical use must be distinguished from the ancient well-frame analysis: the central dot is not presented as a piece of food in a rice bowl, and the old pictograph does not establish the origin of the Japanese bowl name.
