斑
Kun: madara, wakeru  |  On: han
mottled ・ spotted ・ variegated ・ mixed in colour

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 斑 as a compound involving two jades and 文, a patterned marking. The two jade pieces have colours that mingle or contrast, producing a variegated appearance. This is his concrete explanation of mottling.

He argues for 斑 as the proper writing and questions a traditional explanation through a different phonetic associated with judicial contention. In his account the visible patterning of the paired jades gives the relevant sense directly.

From that mingling of colours come spotted, patchy, mixed, and irregular appearances. Shirakawa places the word in a sound-family including 班 and 頒, with associations of distribution and intermixture, and also compares words for beautifully interwoven colour. The character's ordinary descriptive meanings thus continue the patterned combination that he recognizes in its formation.
