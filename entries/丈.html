丈
Kun: take  |  On: jo
cane (original) ・ unit of length ・ ten shaku

Explanation

丈 combines a staff with 又, a hand gripping it. The cross-like member is the staff itself. The character is the early writing of 杖, before the wood element was added to distinguish the object.

A staff of established length could serve as a measuring rod. 丈 therefore names a measure of ten 尺 and the act of taking a length with that rod. Early texts use it for measuring a city wall and for the space to be allowed between mats. Its physical length must be understood within the particular historical system of measures.

The word also concerns a person's stature. In 丈夫 it belongs to adult male standing, and 丈人 can address an older or respected man. These uses connect the measuring staff, full bodily height, and mature status.
