子
Kun: ko, ne  |  On: shi, su
child ・ prince ・ man ・ lord ・ teacher ・ first earthly branch (rat)

Explanation

子 pictures a young child. The early forms emphasize the head and small body. Some show one hand raised and the other lowered, a gesture identifying a child of royal rank. In those contexts, 子 functions as a princely title.

The title survives in names such as 子鄭 and 子雀, probably referring to princes associated with particular territories. Its use in courtesy names and respectful forms of address later broadened beyond that early setting. A respected teacher or learned person could be called 子, ‘Master,’ and the word could address someone as ‘you.’

The ordinary child sense extends to offspring, seeds, eggs, and small things. The same writing also supplies the first name in the twelve-branch cycle, a further use of the established child-sign.
