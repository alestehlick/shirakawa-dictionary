有
Kun: aru  |  On: yuu, u
to have ・ to exist

Explanation

有 combines 又, a right hand, with 肉, flesh, written in the form 月. It shows meat held in the hand and presented to a god. The graph is the earlier form of 侑, offering or urging food upon someone.

Holding the sacrificial meat gives the broader senses ‘hold,’ ‘possess,’ and ‘have.’ What is held or present can also be said to exist, so 有 becomes a general word for being there.

The early writing reflects this close relation between hand and possession: oracle-bone inscriptions can use the hand graph 又 alone for ‘have,’ while bronze inscriptions use the fuller 有. The flesh makes the original act of offering visible.
