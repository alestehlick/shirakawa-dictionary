璧
Uncategorized
Kun: tama  |  On: heki
ritual jade disc ・ precious jade ・ disc-shaped treasure

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 璧 as semantic–phonetic: 玉 identifies jade, and 辟 supplies the sound heki. His analysis of 辟 involves a curved blade cutting a rounded piece from the waist, a form whose circular detail he notes in bronze writing. The completed jade graph names a round disc with a comparatively small central opening.

He then follows the object into ritual use. The Zhouli assigns a dark or blue-green jade bi to the worship of Heaven, and such discs also served in formal exchanges between rulers. Finds from Fu Hao's tomb and offerings recorded on bronze vessels give archaeological and inscriptional context to these uses.

For Shirakawa, jade was an implement for invigorating spiritual power. The character's primary meaning is accordingly a particular valuable ritual object, from which disc-like shape and exceptional beauty become extended senses. Its meaning should remain distinct from 壁, a wall, despite their common phonetic.
