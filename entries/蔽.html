蔽
Kun: oou, kakusu, kurai, fusagu  |  On: hei
to cover ・ to conceal ・ to block ・ obscure

Explanation

蔽 is semantic–phonetic, with 敝 supplying the sound hei. Shirakawa interprets 敝 through an embroidered ceremonial apron covering the knees, connecting it with the wider vocabulary of ritual dress.

Covering something as the apron covers the body gives concealment and obstruction. Something screened from view becomes obscure, and a covering can also separate or block passage. These form the main sequence of meaning in his account.

The Shuowen's explanation through small plants follows an older commentary on a Book of Odes expression; Shirakawa instead makes the covering garment central. Other recorded meanings, including falling or determining something, involve interchange with related writings. They should not all be forced into a botanical origin merely because the received character carries 艸.
