開
Kun: hiraku, hirakeru, aku, akeru  |  On: kai
open ・ to open

Explanation

開 combines 閂, a gate secured by a horizontal bar, with 廾, two hands placed side by side. The hands remove the crossbar and open the two leaves of the gate. The short stroke within 門 is the bar that had held them shut.

From opening the gate, 開 extends to opening or releasing something generally and allowing passage through it. An undertaking can also open or begin, and an event can be convened. Something opened out spreads or expands. These senses all continue the movement from a closed condition into one that permits activity or access.
