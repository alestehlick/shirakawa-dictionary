哺
Kun: fukumu, hagukumu  |  On: ho
to hold food in the mouth ・ to feed ・ to nourish ・ food in the mouth

Explanation

哺 is semantic–phonetic: 口 places the word in the mouth, while 甫 supplies the sound ho. The older explanation concerns chewing or holding food in the mouth in order to give it to a child.

That feeding action provides the transition to rearing and nourishment, as in 哺育. The word can also name the food still held in the mouth, so its noun and verb uses refer to different aspects of the same scene.

Shirakawa also records eating or sipping and an evening-meal usage. His main unfolding, however, stays with food taken into the mouth and passed on to sustain another life. The modern general sense of feeding grows from that more specific bodily act.
