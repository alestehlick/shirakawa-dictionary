侶
Kun: tomo, tomogara  |  On: ryo
companion ・ partner ・ associate ・ to accompany

Explanation

Shirakawa analyzes 侶 as semantic–phonetic, with 人 supplying the human category and 呂 the sound. In his account 呂 pictures pieces of copper or similar metal stock, alike in material and quantity. Their juxtaposition also suggests comparable or corresponding things.

When the person classifier is added, the word concerns people who stand alongside one another as equals: fellows, companions, and partners. The old dictionary gloss 徒侶 names precisely such a company of associates. Accompanying someone and treating someone as a companion follow from that relation.

Shirakawa places 侶 beside 閭 and 旅 in a shared sound-and-meaning field. 閭 concerns people sharing a settlement gate; 旅 concerns a clan group moving together beneath its banner. These comparisons help distinguish companionship as membership and association from a simplistic modern picture of two mouths. The graphic analysis follows the older reading of 呂 and the human relationship expressed by 侶.
