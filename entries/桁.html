桁
Kun: keta, koromokake  |  On: kou
horizontal beam ・ clothes rack ・ wooden restraint ・ numerical place in Japanese

Explanation

桁 is semantic–phonetic, with 木 and 行 as the phonetic kō. Shirakawa associates 行 here with things arranged in a line. The older definition names a roof timber, a beam laid horizontally across supports.

The same word can describe a clothes rack or a wooden restraint. Related writings extend to a bridge made by placing boats alongside one another, retaining the idea of something set across or in an aligned series.

Japanese also uses 桁 for a place on an abacus and hence a numerical digit's position. Shirakawa identifies that specifically as a Japanese application. His comparison with 衡 and 横 places the main physical meanings within a sound-family of crosswise members and things stretched horizontally.
