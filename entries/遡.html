遡
Kun: sakanoboru  |  On: so
to go upstream ・ to go back in time ・ to trace backward ・ to face toward

Explanation

遡 is semantic–phonetic, with 朔 supplying the sound. Shirakawa compares it with 泝, treated as the principal writing in the older dictionary tradition, while observing that both forms were used.

Their conventions differ. 泝 is used for travelling against a watercourse, whereas 遡 is also used for going backward in time. His example is 遡及, where a law's effect reaches back to a time before its promulgation; that expression is not ordinarily written with 泝.

The range also includes approaching or facing something, and a complaint sense through interchange with a related graph. The important distinction is between shared sound and meaning on one hand and the later distribution of the written forms on the other.
