眉
Kun: mayu  |  On: bi, mi
eyebrow ・ long-lived person ・ edge or margin ・ to charm in related usage

Explanation

眉 pictures an eye with an eyebrow above it. Shirakawa reads the ornamentation in oracle-bone forms as a deliberately enhanced brow with ritual power, comparable to a magically charged eye. He therefore goes beyond the older explanation of ordinary hair and forehead wrinkles.

He connects this formation with 媚 and the ritual specialist who exercises a bewitching influence. Related characters concerned with inspection likewise preserve the decorated eye in his analysis.

Long eyebrows were also regarded as signs of longevity, explaining 眉寿 and the use of 眉 for an aged or long-lived person. Other sound-related writings concern borders and margins, including the upper margin of a page. Shirakawa distinguishes these related-word uses from the central pictorial origin in the eye and its brow.
