挨
Kun: utsu  |  On: ai
to push ・ to strike from behind ・ to press close ・ to advance in order

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 挨 as semantic–phonetic. The hand component identifies an action, and 矣 supplies the pronunciation; related graphs such as 埃 show the ai value associated with that phonetic. The Shuowen's gloss describes striking someone's back, giving the action a precise bodily setting.

From a blow or forceful pressure from behind, the word extends to pushing forward or pushing something aside. Close physical contact then provides the connection with approaching, pressing near, and standing or moving in succession. These meanings explain why the character belongs naturally beside other verbs for jostling and pressing.

In the familiar compound 挨拶, its present association with greetings should therefore be read through that history of approach and pressure. The etymological starting point is the hand's action, supported by the old dictionary gloss, rather than the polite exchange suggested by the modern Japanese compound alone.
