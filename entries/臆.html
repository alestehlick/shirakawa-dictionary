臆
Uncategorized
Kun: omou  |  On: oku
breast ・ inner thoughts ・ to conjecture ・ to suppose

Explanation

Shirakawa treats 臆 as semantic–phonetic. The body component identifies its anatomical field, and 意 supplies the sound oku. The Shuowen records a different principal writing with 乙, interpreted as a bone-shaped element, and gives the meaning breastbone; 臆 is listed as an alternative.

The concrete starting point is therefore the chest or breast. From the interior of the breast, the word comes to express what one holds inwardly: thoughts, intentions, and suppositions. The compound 胸臆 makes that connection between the bodily location and one's inner feelings explicit.

Shirakawa also reads 意 through apprehending a divine intention from the sound marking a god's arrival. Within 臆, this helps explain the vocabulary of estimating or conjecturing what is not directly known. The account joins an old anatomical word to the semantic field of inward thought; it does not make the modern emotion of timidity the graph's primary pictorial meaning.
