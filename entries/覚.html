覚
Kun: oboeru, samasu, sameru, mezameru, satoru  |  On: kaku
to awaken ・ to perceive ・ to learn ・ to remember

Explanation

覚, formerly 覺, places 見 beneath the roof-and-hands element also found in 學, ‘learn.’ The upper part represents a place of instruction, with crossed timbers and hands guiding the activity. Adding 子, a child, produces 學; adding 見 produces 覺.

見 shows a person with a large eye. Here it brings seeing and perception into the setting of learning: instruction enables someone to notice and understand. 覚 means to become aware, recognize, or grasp something. The same awakening of awareness applies to waking from sleep. In Japanese, remembering or learning something so that it stays in the mind also belongs to this awakening of knowledge.
