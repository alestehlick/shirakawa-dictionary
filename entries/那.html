那
Kun: utsukushii, ooi, nanzo  |  On: na, da
that ・ which ・ how ・ abundant ・ graceful

Explanation

The older 那 combines 邑, a settlement, with an older left-hand sound element picturing long cheek hair, abundant and pliant. The graph was used for a place-name, with its sound developing from this older series.

In descriptive expressions such as 阿那 and 猗儺, the word concerns luxuriant flowers and gracefully bending branches. Their abundance, beauty, and suppleness share the qualities associated with the original sound element. The sense ‘many’ also overlaps with 多.

那 was further borrowed for grammatical words: asking why or how, pointing to something as ‘that,’ or adding a question at the end of a sentence. These uses come through sound borrowing. They coexist with the older descriptive vocabulary and the settlement-name use of the graph.
