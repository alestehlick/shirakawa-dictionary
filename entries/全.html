全
Kun: mattaku, subete, mattou  |  On: zen
all ・ whole ・ entire ・ complete

Explanation

An old form of 全 appears to depict a pendant ornament of jade, with additional pieces hanging from it. This identification remains tentative. The received shape resembles 入 over 玉, retaining the association with jade and completeness.

A complete ornament provides a possible visual basis for something being intact and fully furnished. From this come ‘whole,’ ‘entire,’ ‘all present,’ and preserving something in its entirety. The character can describe both the whole of a thing and the condition in which none of its required parts is missing.

The similar shape of 金 belongs to a different material history, involving metal cast into a form.
