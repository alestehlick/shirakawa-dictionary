餅
Kun: mochi  |  On: hei
cake of kneaded flour ・ mochi ・ rice cake ・ flat cake-like object

Explanation

Shirakawa treats 餅, also written 餠, as semantic–phonetic. 食 identifies food, and the component associated with 并 or joining supplies the sound hei. He begins with the Shuowen's explanation of a food made from wheat flour: the flour is kneaded together, formed, and steamed.

The Shiming connects the name with bringing the dough into one joined mass. Shirakawa develops this as combining material into a single flat piece. The cake's coherent, flattened shape then allows the word to name other things of similar form, including a cake-shaped piece of precious metal.

The Japanese use for a cake made from glutinous rice is a particular development of this older food word. Following the historical sequence keeps the Chinese wheat-flour preparation, the general flat-cake shape, and the Japanese rice-based mochi meaning distinct but connected.
