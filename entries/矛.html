矛
Kun: hoko  |  On: mu, bou
spear ・ long-handled weapon ・ to assail

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 矛 as a pictograph of a long-handled spear. The old descriptions distinguish named kinds by their length and by the presence of an additional projecting blade, the latter belonging to the 戟 type. The essential object is the weapon on its long shaft.

He follows its use beyond hand-to-hand fighting into the equipment of a war chariot. A spear could be fixed upright on a support and displayed as the ruler's chariot made a circuit to inspect and pacify the territory. A bronze-bell inscription recording a royal inspection supplies the historical context he discusses.

The ordinary spear meaning and the extended verbal sense of attacking retain the implement's practical force. Its ceremonial display also shows how a recognizable weapon could serve as a sign of authority during movement through a ruler's domain.
