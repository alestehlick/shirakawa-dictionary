邪
Kun: yokoshima  |  On: ja
evil ・ wrong ・ wicked

Explanation

邪 combines 邑, a settlement, with 牙 as its phonetic. 牙 pictures large, curved animal fangs and carries an association with crookedness or an irregular form. Applied to the settlement, it probably concerns a city wall that does not make a regular square.

From being out of the proper alignment come obliqueness, deviation, and what is wrong. The related words 邪 and 斜 share this movement between physical crookedness and a departure from what is considered correct.

邪 therefore describes perversity, deceit, and harmful or malign influences. It can name an evil spirit as well as a distorted intention. Other uses, including the sentence-final question later written 耶, belong to sound-based grammatical interchange.
