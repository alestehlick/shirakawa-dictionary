股
Kun: mata  |  On: ko
thigh ・ groin ・ branch ・ part or share

Explanation

The body element 肉 identifies the region of meaning in 股. Shirakawa questions treating the remaining 殳 simply as an ordinary phonetic, because its usual sound does not adequately explain ko. He instead considers it an abbreviation of a fuller phonetic graph.

His reconstruction connects that fuller form with castrating a ram: the genitals between the legs are cut using the implement represented by 殳. On this account the graphic history points to the groin and the adjoining thighs. This is a proposed reconstruction of the fuller formation, not a picture of a modern leg in 股 itself.

The word subsequently denotes a thigh, a branching division, or one part of a whole. In 股肱, thigh and upper arm represent indispensable supporting limbs and hence a ruler's trusted assistants.
