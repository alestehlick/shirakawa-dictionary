阪
Kun: saka, tsutsumi, kewashii  |  On: han
slope ・ steep bank ・ embankment ・ barrier

Explanation

Shirakawa treats 阪 as semantic–phonetic, with 反 providing the sound and 阜 the semantic setting. He associates 反 with turning back from terrain difficult to climb. In his interpretation 阜 originates as a sacred ladder, a place where a deity ascends and descends.

阪 consequently evokes an elevated sacred location that ordinary people cannot readily climb into. Such a place might also be protected by a barrier; Shirakawa compares it cautiously with a Japanese iwakura, a rock-associated sacred site. The old dictionary senses include a steep bank, a mountain's flank, and an embankment separating one place from another.

The later spelling 坂 uses 土 and comes to refer more generally to a slope in the terrain. The relationship explains their interchange without erasing Shirakawa's more particular account of 阪. Its development runs from a difficult, separated elevation to the ordinary vocabulary of slopes, inclines, and banks.
