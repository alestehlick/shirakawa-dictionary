睦
Kun: mutsumu, shitashimu, yawaragu  |  On: boku
to live in harmony ・ to be intimate ・ gentle ・ sincere

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 睦 as semantic–phonetic, using the phonetic also found in 陸. He explains the difference between its r-like and m-like readings through an established interchange of those initial sound classes. The modern sound boku should therefore not force an unrelated component story.

The older glosses concern an obedient or agreeable gaze and a respectful harmony. A ritual commentary specifies closeness among one's extended kin, providing the social setting for friendship, familial concord, and intimacy.

睦 also overlaps in sound and usage with 穆, and through that relation can mean sincerity. Shirakawa interprets 穆 through grain becoming full before it splits open. In the present entry that comparison supports the word-family; the ordinary development of 睦 remains harmonious relations, gentleness, and quiet mutual regard.
