脊
Kun: se, sebone  |  On: seki
backbone ・ spine ・ back ・ central ridge

Explanation

Shirakawa analyzes 脊 as a compound of an older graph for the backbone or back portion and 肉, flesh. The older definitions likewise place the word in the back and vertebral region. The modern shape is best understood through that earlier bodily formation.

His discussion follows the word into sacrificial practice. Meat from the back formed a valued principal portion presented in ritual, so the backbone was significant both as an anatomical support and as the central part of an offering.

The linked series of vertebrae then provides an image for a connecting axis or main ridge. 脊梁 expresses the supporting backbone, and mountain ridges can be described through the same bodily comparison. The later figurative senses preserve the idea of a strong central line joining and supporting the whole.
