釜
Kun: kama  |  On: fu
cooking pot ・ cauldron ・ measuring vessel ・ unit of capacity

Explanation

釜 is semantic–phonetic, with 父 supplying the sound fu. Shirakawa points to bronze inscriptions whose fuller writings combine the vessel classifier 缶 with this phonetic; another traditional fuller form is later.

The named vessels from Qi served as measures. Their physical form is broad-bellied, flat-bottomed, and without legs. A classical list of capacity units confirms this measuring function, and 釜 can name both the container and its capacity.

In western usage it was also a cooking vessel. Shirakawa cites a Book of Odes poem about boiling water plants for an ancestral offering. The history therefore includes regional differences in use: a vessel for measurement in one setting and for preparing food in another, rather than one exclusively modern kitchen meaning.
