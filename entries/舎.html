舎
Kun: suteru, oku, yadoru, ie  |  On: sha, seki
lodging ・ house ・ building ・ to stay ・ to set aside

Explanation

For 舎, also written 舍, Shirakawa begins with a handled digging or piercing implement and 口, the sai containing a written prayer. The long implement penetrates the receptacle and disables the prayer's efficacy. He therefore identifies discarding or setting aside as the early meaning, with 舍 serving as the original writing later differentiated as 捨.

From giving something up or stopping an action, the word develops the senses of leaving something in place, remaining, and lodging. A building used for that stay is consequently a 舎. Shirakawa treats the Shuowen's explanation as a dwelling in a market town as a description of a later sense, not the original composition.

Bronze inscriptions also use the graph for issuing a command and bestowing goods. His account preserves those established uses alongside the progression from setting aside to stopping and staying. The modern building vocabulary is one branch of this older range of actions.
