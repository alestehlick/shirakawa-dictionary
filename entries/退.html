退
Kun: shirizoku, shirizokeru  |  On: tai
to retreat ・ to withdraw ・ to recede

Explanation

退's older formation combines 彳, movement along a road, with a 日-shaped abbreviation of a ritual food vessel and 夊, a backward-facing foot. The vessel holds the offering, and the reversed foot marks its removal.

When worship is over, the food presented to the deity is taken back. This withdrawal gives drawing away, stepping back, and moving something out of the place it occupied. Yielding or moving aside also belongs to respectful movement during a ceremony.

The uses broaden to retreating, resigning, stopping, or causing someone to withdraw. Early military advance and retreat could be decided through other divinatory rites; the general vocabulary later converges in 退. The offering removed after worship remains the concrete starting action.
