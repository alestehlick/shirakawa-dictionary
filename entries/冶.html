冶
Kun: tokasu, iru, tsukuru, namamekashii  |  On: ya
to melt metal ・ to cast or work metal ・ metalworker ・ alluring or beautiful

Explanation

冶 is semantic–phonetic, with 台 supplying the relevant sound. Shirakawa points to the additional i-like readings represented by 怡 and 詒 to clarify this phonetic relationship.

Although the received character is classified under the ice element, he finds that the bronze forms instead use the shape of a mass of molten metal, also present in other metal-related graphs. Melting and casting metal, rather than chilling it, therefore organize his account.

The worked metal produces a brilliant surface, giving a route from smelting and refining to radiant beauty and allure, as in 艶冶. The word can also name the craft or its practitioner. Shirakawa compares related melting words while keeping their different graphic histories distinct.
