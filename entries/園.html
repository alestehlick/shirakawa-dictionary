園
Kun: sono  |  On: en
garden ・ park ・ cemetery

Explanation

園 places the sound element 袁 within 囗, an enclosure. 袁 shows a dead person’s garment with jade placed at the neckline. Above it, a footprint marks departure: the dead person is being sent on the journey to the other world. This is also the earlier graph behind 遠.

With that funerary meaning, 園 probably first names the planting or grove within a burial ground. Trees were planted in the enclosed precinct, and words for tombs and mausoleum grounds preserve this setting.

From the planted grave precinct, 園 broadens to a cultivated enclosure: a garden, orchard, or park. The enclosure and planting remain, while the use no longer has to be funerary.
