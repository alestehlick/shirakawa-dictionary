鬱
Kun: shigeru, fusagaru, ureeru  |  On: utsu
dense growth ・ pent-up ・ stifled ・ gloom ・ dejection

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 鬱 as a compound involving 林, a vessel, a cover, 鬯, and the strokes 彡. 鬯 belongs to brewing fragrant wine, while 彡 represents its rising aroma. His starting scene is a covered vessel with aromatic herbs added, kept enclosed while the brew matures.

The vessel becomes filled with fragrance and brewing vapour. This gives a route to being enclosed, stopped up, or filled with something that cannot freely escape. Applied to the heart, the same condition becomes pent-up feeling: grief, resentment, anger, or a knot of emotion.

The lush-growth sense belongs to interchange with a related plant word, whose writing came to share 鬱. Shirakawa therefore keeps both fragrant brewing and dense vegetation in view, while giving the enclosed maturation process a central role in the character's development. The modern emotional meaning continues the image of a condition held inside.
