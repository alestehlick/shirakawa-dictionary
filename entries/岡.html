岡
Uncategorized
Kun: oka  |  On: kou
fired earth ・ red or bare hill ・ hill ・ ridge

Explanation

Shirakawa interprets 岡 as a casting mould, represented by a net-like outline, with fire used to bake it. He rejects the ordinary analysis through 山 and the definition mountain ridge as an adequate account of the formation.

The related 剛 depicts cutting or splitting the hardened mould to remove it. The fired material is red, and Shirakawa connects this colour with another word in the same phonetic family for a red ox.

岡 then describes a hill whose red earth resembles baked soil, especially bare ground without vegetation. Hill, slope, summit, and ridge broaden that terrain meaning. In this account the visible modern mountain-like strokes belong to an older fire-and-mould formation; the earthen colour supplies the bridge to the familiar landscape word.
