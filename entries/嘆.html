嘆
Uncategorized
Kun: nageku, nagekawashii, itamu  |  On: tan
to sigh ・ to lament ・ to exclaim ・ to admire or praise

Explanation

Shirakawa treats 嘆 as semantic–phonetic, with 口 indicating vocal expression and the remaining older element supplying the relevant sound. That element also belongs to the formation of 歎, a closely related writing.

He interprets its older graphic base through the burning of a ritual medium during famine. Within his account, the desperate rite and its suffering provide the background to the cry of grief. The modern components alone conceal this proposed ritual history.

The word covers a deep sigh, lamentation, and the release or expression of sorrow. Vocal outpouring can also convey admiration, so 嘆 and 歎 extend into exclamation and praise. The movement is from a forceful emotional utterance to its different emotional settings; the positive praise meaning does not require replacing the older lament with an unrelated origin.
