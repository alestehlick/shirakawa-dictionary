麓
Kun: fumoto, yamamori  |  On: roku
foot of a mountain ・ wooded foothills ・ forest warden

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 麓 as semantic–phonetic: 林 identifies woodland, and 鹿 supplies the sound roku. The old explanations concern both the woodland adjoining a mountain and the official who guards such woodland.

He connects these senses with enclosed forest or parkland established where trees grow thickly at a mountain's foot. A classical passage names the official responsible for the mountain forest, preserving the administrative side of the word.

The resulting range includes foothills, a large wooded area, and its keeper. Older variant forms occur in oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions as personal names; Shirakawa treats their relation through their shared sound. The familiar Japanese fumoto is consequently one part of a history joining the wooded lower slopes with the land and office associated with them.
