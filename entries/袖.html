袖
Kun: sode  |  On: shuu
sleeve ・ sleeve opening ・ to put or hide in a sleeve

Explanation

袖 combines 衣 with 由 as the phonetic shū, a reading also found in 岫. Shirakawa follows it through a fuller older writing using a different, grain-related phonetic, and through an intermediate Han form that shows the transition toward 由.

He rejects making the older graph a purely descriptive image of clothing hanging like grain. The history is principally a change in phonetic construction.

A later explanation calls the sleeve the place through which the hand passes in and out. Shirakawa compares 岫, a rocky hollow, and a word-family associated with an empty interior. The sleeve is thus named as a hollow passage in the garment. It can also become a verb for placing or concealing something inside that space.
