重
Kun: omoi, omonjiru, kasaneru, kasanaru, e  |  On: juu, chou
heavy ・ weight ・ to pile up ・ layered ・ important

Explanation

重 combines 東 with 土. 東 originally pictures a sack tied at both ends. 土 is placed beneath it like an added weight, making the burden of the sack explicit. The graph means ‘heavy’ or ‘having weight.’

The related 量 adds an opening for pouring at the top of the sack and concerns measuring its contents, especially grain. 重 emphasizes their weight.

From physical heaviness come pressure and accumulated weight, then things piled in layers or added one upon another. Applied to conduct or judgement, weight becomes gravity and importance. Something valued highly is treated as weighty; a dignified person carries that weight in a measured, serious manner.
