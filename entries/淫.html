淫
Kun: midara, habikoru, fukeru  |  On: in
excessive ・ to spread unchecked ・ to indulge ・ licentious

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 淫 as semantic–phonetic and begins with water penetrating along the channels or texture of the ground. The Shuowen's explanation concerns infiltration and also prolonged rain: moisture continuing to enter and spread without stopping.

He reads the phonetic element as a hand pressing or urging a person who stands presenting a prayer. In his interpretation this contributes the sense of pressing beyond the ordinary measure, or excess. With the water component, that excess appears as soaking, flooding, or pervasive moisture.

The same idea then applies more generally to something continuing beyond due bounds: prolonged duration, unchecked growth, indulgence, and excessive desire. Shirakawa distinguishes the water word from 婬 when specifically discussing human sexual desire. The ethical and sexual uses thus belong to the wider development of excess, while the concrete water-and-permeation senses remain essential to his account.
