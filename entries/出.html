出
Kun: deru, dasu, izuru, idasu  |  On: shutsu, sui
to go out ・ to leave ・ to depart ・ to put out

Explanation

出 pictures a foot stepping out firmly. Its core is 止, the shape of a footprint, with an extra curve behind the heel marking the strong impression left as the person sets off. The graph gives the movement of departure: going out from where one has been.

Some bronze forms add a vessel containing a prayer. This probably records a departure rite, in which the traveller’s setting out was placed under divine protection.

From leaving a place, 出 extends to advancing, appearing, or emerging into view. Used of making something move outward, it means to take out, send out, or produce. The heel’s departure supplies the same outward movement in each case.
