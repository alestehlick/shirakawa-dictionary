窟
Uncategorized
Kun: ana, iwaya  |  On: kutsu
cave ・ den ・ cavern ・ haunt

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 窟 as semantic–phonetic: 穴 identifies a hollow or underground space, and 屈 supplies the sound. He interprets 屈 itself as an animal bending or curling its tail. Within 窟, that animal association gives a natural setting to the dwelling named by the whole graph: the hole or cave in which a creature lives.

The sense broadens from an animal's den to a cave used as a human dwelling. Shirakawa also notes its use for underground burial chambers. These are developments of the same enclosed, excavated space; the character does not require a separate pictorial origin for each use.

From a place occupied by animals or people, 窟 can further denote a gathering place, stronghold, or habitual haunt. His example 遊仙窟 illustrates its application to a pleasure quarter. The figurative sense remains grounded in the concrete idea of an inhabited recess.
