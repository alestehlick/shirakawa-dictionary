嗅
Kun: kagu  |  On: kyuu
to smell ・ to sniff ・ to give off an odour

Explanation

Shirakawa treats 嗅 as an abbreviation of a fuller graph combining 鼻, nose, with 臭, odour. That fuller formation joins the organ to what it approaches and perceives. The older explanation describes bringing the nose toward a smell; 臭 also participates in the sound.

The modern 口 form therefore needs comparison with its fuller predecessor before its formation becomes clear. Its principal use is verbal: to smell or sniff, with an additional lexical sense of being odorous.

Shirakawa relates it to words for odour and putrefaction. He also questions a famous Analects occurrence apparently describing a bird sniffing before flight, proposing a textual confusion with a word for spreading the wings. That passage should not be used uncritically as evidence for an unusual original meaning of 嗅.
