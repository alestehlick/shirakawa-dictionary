剝
Uncategorized
Kun: hagasu, hagu, hagareru, hageru, saku  |  On: haku
to skin ・ to peel ・ to strip off ・ to flake or fall away

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 剝 as a compound of an animal form and 刀. He cautions that the left element is not the identically shaped graph associated with drilling; its seal form instead resembles an animal, whose hide is removed with a knife.

An older variant combines bone and knife, supporting the cutting and stripping action. The primary range includes splitting, skinning, peeling, and scraping away a surface.

What is stripped can be forcibly taken from someone, giving 剝奪; a surface can also detach by itself, giving 剝落. Shirakawa compares sound-related impact words and regards the spoken word as imitating the noise of stripping. The graphic action and the proposed sound imitation contribute different parts of his explanation.
