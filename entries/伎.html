伎
Kun: waza  |  On: ki, gi
performing art ・ skill ・ performer ・ bodily performance

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 伎 as semantic–phonetic, with 人 marking the human field and 支 supplying the sound. He examines the older glosses for a companion or associate, but finds their supporting examples unconvincing for that particular meaning.

He instead follows the word through descriptions of a performer's tilted or shifting posture and bodily movement. Related vocabulary concerns the bearing of dancers, and he compares the expressive movement to Japanese kabuku. The important action is the person adopting a distinctive performing stance or movement.

From this bodily performance come entertainment, music and dance, the performer, and the skill exercised in the performance. The related graphs 技 and 妓 share the sound, while giving other classifiers to the field of ability and musical entertainment. His account thus develops the artistic meanings through the moving human body and its associated vocabulary.
