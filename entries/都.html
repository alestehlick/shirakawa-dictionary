都
Kun: miyako  |  On: to, tsu
capital ・ metropolis ・ all ・ elegance

Explanation

都 combines 邑, written 阝 at the right, with the sound element 者. 邑 shows people within an enclosure and means a settlement. 者 pictures branches piled over 曰, a vessel containing a prayer, then covered with earth. This makes the protected earthen boundary later written 堵.

The prayer buried in the earthwork placed the boundary under sacred protection, while the wall also guarded against intrusion. 都 names a large settlement surrounded by such an enclosure. From the fortified settlement, it comes to mean a capital or the city in which the ruler resides.

The capital’s concentration of people and activity gives further uses for gathering and abundance. Its cultivated life also supplies the sense of elegance or refinement—the polished manner associated with the city.
