畿
Uncategorized
Kun: miyako  |  On: ki
capital ・ royal domain ・ boundary ・ cultivated territory

Explanation

畿 is semantic–phonetic, with 幾 supplying the sound ki. Shirakawa interprets 幾 as a spear, 戈, fitted with thread or silk as a ritual ornament. The implement is used in an inspecting and purifying action.

In 畿 that action concerns cultivated territory: the fields and inhabited area are ritually purified. The word can name the settlement gate, the region organized around it, and eventually the capital itself.

畿内 then denotes the territory under the sovereign's direct control. The senses boundary and cultivated land retain aspects of the area being defined and protected. In Shirakawa's unfolding, the political geography grows from ritual treatment of the land; the character is not simply an abstract combination meaning fields near a capital.
