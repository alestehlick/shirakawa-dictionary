多
Kun: ooi, amata  |  On: ta
many ・ much ・ numerous

Explanation

多 places one 夕-shaped piece of meat above another. Here the forms represent flesh prepared for an offering. Two portions together show an abundance of sacrificial meat.

The same stacked meat appears in early forms of 宜, where it is set on an offering stand before the ancestral sanctuary. 多 takes the plentiful contents of the offering as its meaning.

From much meat, the character broadens to a large amount or number of anything. Abundance can also mean more than something else, giving ‘exceed’ or ‘excel.’ To regard someone’s contribution as substantial becomes valuing or praising it. The ordinary ‘many’ begins with the plentiful sacred provision.
