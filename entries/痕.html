痕
Kun: ato, kizuato  |  On: kon
scar ・ mark left by a wound ・ trace

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 痕 as semantic–phonetic: 疒 places the word among bodily afflictions, and 艮 supplies the sound kon. He interprets 艮 through stopping or recoiling before a hostile gaze, rather than through its familiar modern meaning good. Related graphs also connect this sound element with the heel and a person's tracks.

The old glosses of 痕 concern a scar or the hardened mark remaining after injury. Shirakawa includes the traces produced by cutting and marking the body in explaining this bodily starting point. The emphasis is on what remains visible after the original action.

From a wound's lasting mark, the word extends to a trace of something that has passed. The bodily scar and the more general evidence of a former presence remain connected by this idea of a surviving imprint.
