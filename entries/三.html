三
Kun: mi, mitsu, mittsu  |  On: san
three

Explanation

三 records three horizontal counting marks. The early numeral system repeats the same simple stroke for the first few numbers, making the quantity directly visible. Three marks give the number three without a pictorial scene.

Later natural philosophy treated one, two, and three as successive stages in the unfolding of the world, with three completing heaven, earth, and human beings. That scheme gives an existing numeral a cosmological significance. Three consequently becomes a favoured sacred number and a basis for many named groups.

參, now 参, is sometimes used in numerical writing because of its related sound. Its own image is three hairpins gathered in a coiffure. The numeral 三 and the ornamental graph 參 thus meet through sound and use, while retaining distinct graphic origins.
