頃
Kun: koro, katamuku, shibaraku  |  On: kei, kyou
to incline in reverence ・ a short while ・ around a time ・ a land measure

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 頃 as a compound of a descending spirit, represented by 匕, and 頁, a person prepared for ritual. The human figure bends in reverence before the arriving presence. He regards 頃 as an initial writing of 傾.

Related forms add the invocation vessel and connect this posture with 稽首, the formal bow, and with 詣, divine arrival. The older definition of an unstraight head thus refers, in his account, to reverent inclination rather than an accidental crooked posture.

Because the divine appearance lasts only briefly, 頃 comes to denote a short interval, then a time or period more generally. Shirakawa records the separate land-measure use as well. The main unfolding joins the bowing posture, the brief encounter, and the temporal word.
