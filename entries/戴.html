戴
Uncategorized
Kun: itadaku  |  On: tai
to bear on the head ・ to raise respectfully ・ to receive reverently ・ to honour

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 戴 as semantic–phonetic and identifies an older sound value in the upper enclosing element. He associates that element with adding ritual ornament and thereby consecrating something. The sound relationship is historical and is not evident from the usual modern Japanese readings of every component.

He also interprets 異 in the composition through a strange or spirit-bearing head, an object or figure invested with extraordinary power. To support or raise it respectfully gives the central action of 戴.

The word consequently expresses lifting something above oneself, bearing it on the head, and receiving it with reverence. From the raised and honoured position come venerating, welcoming, and acknowledging a superior. The familiar Japanese itadaku preserves that respectful reception. Shirakawa's unfolding joins the graph's consecrating association with the action of raising and sustaining what is honoured.
