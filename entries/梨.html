梨
Kun: nashi  |  On: ri
pear ・ pear tree ・ the theatrical world in 梨園

Explanation

Shirakawa treats 梨 as semantic–phonetic, with 木 identifying the tree and a fuller ri phonetic behind the simpler received writing. The older definition names the pear fruit, admired for its sweetness and crispness.

He follows the tree into Tang and Song poems celebrating pear blossoms. A different cultural development concerns the 梨園, the Pear Garden in the imperial grounds where Emperor Xuanzong had performers trained. That place-name later came to denote the world of theatrical performance.

Other recorded senses involve sound-based interchange with words for age, splitting, or the multitude. They should not be presented as physical transformations of the pear. The main sequence is the fruit-bearing tree, its flowers, and the historical institution named after a pear garden.
