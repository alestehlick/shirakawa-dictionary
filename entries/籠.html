籠
Kun: kago, komoru, komeru  |  On: rou
basket ・ cage ・ to enclose ・ to remain inside

Explanation

Shirakawa analyzes 籠 as semantic–phonetic, with 竹 giving the material category and 龍 supplying the sound. The Shuowen's first concrete object is a basket used for carrying earth. Another recorded sense is a container for arrows, a quiver.

Those objects share their function: things are collected and held inside them. Shirakawa follows that enclosing action into the wider meanings of gathering together, including, and shutting something in. Japanese komoru, remaining within, continues this sense of containment.

The same idea can be applied to people or affairs. 籠統 concerns taking things together within one compass, while 籠絡 describes bringing someone under one's influence or control. These figurative uses grow out of the basket's capacity to hold its contents. His explanation moves from a bamboo container to the action and condition of being enclosed.
