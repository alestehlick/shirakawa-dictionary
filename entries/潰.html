潰
Uncategorized
Kun: tsubusu, tsubureru, tsuieru  |  On: kai
to burst through ・ to collapse ・ to scatter in defeat ・ to ulcerate

Explanation

潰 combines water with 貴 as its phonetic. Shirakawa notes kai-type readings in this phonetic family and a recurrent association with collapse or disorder, while acknowledging that the exact relation to 貴 is unclear.

He interprets the older gloss leak as water breaking through containment, especially a breached embankment, rather than merely a dripping roof. From the failure of a containing barrier come collapse, dispersal, flight, and defeat.

The word also overlaps with related writings for an ulcer breaking open and for mental disturbance. Water bursting out and a swelling rupturing share the failure of a boundary, but the sound-family is part of the explanation as well. Shirakawa presents the broader group as related vocabulary without claiming a fully certain graphic derivation for every member.
