埼
Uncategorized
Kun: sai, saki, misaki  |  On: ki
curving shore ・ cape or headland ・ projecting bank ・ mountainside in related usage

Explanation

埼 combines 土 with 奇 as the phonetic ki. Shirakawa interprets 奇 through a curved cutting implement and an association with irregular or uneven shape.

埼 denotes a bend or projection in a waterside bank. A commentary on a classical rhapsody identifies it as the end or head of a curving shore, providing the geographical setting for the word.

Japanese uses it for a cape or headland. Shirakawa distinguishes that use from the older definition of 岬 as a mountain's side, although the writings can converge in Japanese geography. He also records 崎 and 碕 as alternative forms. The different earth, mountain, and stone classifiers specify related landscapes while 奇 maintains the sound and irregular-shape connection.
