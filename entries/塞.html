塞
Uncategorized
Kun: fusagu, fusagaru, toride  |  On: sai, soku
to block ・ to close off ・ to fill ・ frontier fortress

Explanation

Shirakawa works back to an earlier form of 塞 in which the entrance to a building is packed with repeated ritual implements shaped like 工. Their purpose in his interpretation is to seal in dangerous spiritual powers. The added 土 represents the local earth deity.

He places such barriers at roads and strategic border points, where an earth deity is worshipped to prevent alien or harmful spirits from passing. The concrete act is therefore closing and separating a sacred boundary, with the filling of an opening making the barrier effective. He connects this with related boundary vocabulary involving a vessel buried at a sacred site.

The senses to stop, shut, block, or fill continue that sealing action. A fortified frontier is a later military application of the same protective boundary. Shirakawa compares the Japanese sae-no-kami, a deity guarding passage, with this older religious use.
