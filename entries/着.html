着
Kun: kiru, kiseru, tsuku, tsukeru  |  On: chaku, jaku
to wear ・ to put on ・ to arrive ・ to attach

Explanation

着 was originally written 著, with 者 as its sound element. 者 pictures a protective earthen enclosure made with branches and earth. Within it, 曰, vessels holding written prayers, were buried at several points like talismans. Their power was made to adhere to the wall so that it could repel intruders.

This attachment of protective force gives the sense ‘attach’ or ‘adhere.’ Something put on the body is attached to it, giving wearing clothes; someone reaching a destination has come into contact with the place, giving arrival.

着 became the usual form for these everyday meanings. 著 continues chiefly in uses concerning what is conspicuous or set down in writing, while 着 covers attachment, wearing, and reaching a place.
