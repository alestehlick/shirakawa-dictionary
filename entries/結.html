結
Kun: musubu, yuu, yuwaeru  |  On: ketsu
to tie ・ to bind ・ to connect ・ to conclude ・ to promise ・ to strengthen

Explanation

結 joins 糸, thread, with 吉 as the sound element. 吉 pictures a prayer receptacle protected by a small axe-head above it. The covering safeguards what is held inside and seals in the prayer's power.

Tying a knot likewise had a ritual significance: a cord could fasten and preserve something whose effectiveness was to be maintained. Ancient songs give the tying of cords a symbolic role, and congratulatory cords continue that association with binding and good wishes. The character's literal action is fastening threads together.

From tying and tightening come joining, linking, gathering into a bundle, and making a connection secure. A promise or agreement can similarly be made firm, giving uses such as 結約 and 結盟. The binding thread keeps a connection firm, as 吉 safeguards the prayer held within its vessel.
