毀
Kun: yaburu, soshiru  |  On: ki
to strike and destroy ・ to damage ・ to remove calamity ・ to slander

Explanation

Shirakawa reads 毀 as a compound of a young child standing upright and 殳, an implement used to strike. He interprets the head shape through the unclosed cranial sutures also represented in 兒, rather than accepting a primary explanation about breaking pottery.

He proposes that the scene depicts a sacrificial killing intended to remove calamity. Archaeological child burials and a ritual text about rites of averting misfortune provide the context he discusses; the identification remains his reconstruction of the formation.

Destroying or damaging then extends to objects and to a person's reputation. Shirakawa compares 燬, destruction by fire, and 壊 within the related vocabulary. The entry's range also includes bodily wasting during mourning, but his central unfolding runs from ritual violence and removal to general destruction and defamation.
