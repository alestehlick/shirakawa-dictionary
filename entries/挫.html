挫
Kun: kujiku  |  On: za
to crush ・ to frustrate ・ to sprain ・ to humiliate

Explanation

Shirakawa identifies 挫 as semantic–phonetic, combining the hand component with 坐 as the phonetic. 坐 concerns a seated or lowered position and also appears in the vocabulary of being subjected to a legal charge. Those associations give the sound element a connection with being brought low.

The Shuowen's gloss is crushing or breaking down. The action can affect a physical object or a person's standing: something is pressed, damaged, or made to yield, and someone can be humbled or subjected to humiliation.

The familiar senses in 挫折 and 頓挫 continue that loss of forward movement or effective force. In bodily vocabulary the action becomes wrenching or injuring, as in a sprain. Shirakawa's unfolding keeps the hand's forceful action, the phonetic's lowered posture, and the physical or social experience of being brought down in a single connected field.
