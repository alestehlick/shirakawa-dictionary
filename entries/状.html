状
Kun:  |  On: jo
state ・ condition ・ appearance ・ document ・ card

Explanation

状, formerly 狀, combines 犬, a dog, with the sound element 爿. Here 爿 pictures a board used to hold earth in place during rammed-earth construction. The dog probably represents an animal offered when such building work was begun.

The offering would establish the scale and circumstances of the construction and seek a favourable course for it. From the condition of the work—its present state and its expected development—状 came to mean a situation, appearance, or state of affairs. The shape of an object and a person’s outward appearance are further applications of this sense.

A report describes how matters stand. The character consequently also came to name a written statement, note, or document, preserving the act of setting out a situation for someone else to know.
