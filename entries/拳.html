拳
Kun: kobushi, nigiru, utsu  |  On: ken, kan
fist ・ to clench ・ to strike ・ earnest devotion

Explanation

Shirakawa classifies 拳 as semantic–phonetic and examines the fuller seal form through its hand elements and an element associated with an animal's paw. The concrete action is curling the palm inward when preparing to strike. The resulting curled hand is the fist.

He places the word with 卷 and related graphs whose sounds and meanings involve rolling, bending, and enclosing. This makes the clenched form of the hand central to the character's history, alongside its use for striking and the force exerted by a fist.

The repeated expression 拳拳 carries the idea into bodily and emotional conduct. A person bends or holds themselves with respectful care, and the word describes earnest attendance, devotion, or attentive effort. The progression connects a physically curled hand with the restrained, intent posture of someone carefully holding or serving something.
