度
Kun: tabi  |  On: do, to, taku
degree ・ measure ・ times ・ regulation

Explanation

度 combines a shortened form of 席, a mat, with 又, a hand. The gesture is the spreading out of the mat. Its length and breadth provide a standard against which other lengths can be measured.

From this practical standard come measurement, a scale, and the degree or extent of something. Early texts use the verb for surveying woods and measuring land before settling people upon it. A fixed measure also becomes a rule, giving the uses for regulations and institutions.

Other branches include going across or beyond an extent, sometimes written 渡, and counting repeated occasions as so many ‘times.’ The measuring verb has the reading taku, while many nominal and counting uses have do. The abbreviated upper part preserves the mat, 席, spread out by the hand.
