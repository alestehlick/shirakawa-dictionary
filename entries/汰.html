汰
Uncategorized
Kun: nigoru, yonageru  |  On: ta, tai
to wash and sort ・ to sift out ・ surging water ・ excess

Explanation

Shirakawa treats 汰 as semantic–phonetic, with water as the semantic component and 太 supplying the sound. He begins with agitated or surging water and the action of using water to wash materials apart.

Washing grain or separating sand and gravel gives the concrete sense of sorting by a current. This underlies 淘汰 and the older material sense of 沙汰. The unwanted or finer matter is carried away, and what remains has been selected through washing.

Shirakawa places 汰 with 泰 and 太 in a shared sound-and-meaning field. Through that relationship the word can also express going beyond a limit, luxurious excess, or an overflowing condition. His account thus keeps two connected developments in view: water acting on material to separate it, and the broader idea of an action or state exceeding its measure.
