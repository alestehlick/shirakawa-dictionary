草
Kun: kusa, gusa  |  On: sou
grass ・ weeds ・ draft ・ shabby ・ initial

Explanation

草 combines 艸, plants, with 早 as its sound element. 早 originally pictures the scooping part of a spoon. 草 took over the ordinary meaning of the simpler 艸: grass, herbage, or vegetation growing over the ground.

From wild growth, the word also describes what is rough, coarse, or not fully finished. In writing, this becomes a rough draft or rapid, informal script. The first version is set down before it has been worked into a polished form.

The beginning senses connect by sound with 創. 草創 names an undertaking’s first establishment, and 起草 the initial drafting of a text.
