畏
Kun: osoreru, tsutsushimu  |  On: i, wai
to fear ・ to stand in awe ・ to be circumspect ・ to show reverence

Explanation

畏 is pictographic in Shirakawa's account: the early forms show a ghost-headed figure holding a ritual staff and displaying its spiritual power. He rejects the older explanation that takes the lower strokes as tiger claws; oracle-bone and bronze forms instead support a person and a magical staff.

Such a being could appear in dreams and provoke dread. Oracle inscriptions concerning fearful dreams belong to this background. The frightening apparition and the human response to it supply the movement from the figure to fear and awe.

Fear can also impose restraint, producing circumspection, respectful conduct, and formal reverence. Shirakawa compares 畏 with 威, noting extensive interchange in inscriptions and classical texts. Their connection concerns the power that inspires fear and the fear or deference that answers that power.
